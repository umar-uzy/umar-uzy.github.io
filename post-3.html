What Five Years at Amana Taught Me

Amana Bank was my first workplace after school. Five years there, most of it as Head Teller, then moving into corporate account opening, outward clearing, salary and TT payments, foreign currency, business banking. I walked in not really knowing what a work environment even looked like day to day, and looking back, it's the place that shaped me the most — not just in skills, but in how to actually show up and work.

Branch banking teaches you a very specific kind of discipline. A till has to balance exactly at the end of the day — not close, not "probably right." There's no partial credit for a reconciliation that's off by a small amount. You either find the error or you don't go home. I didn't think of it as anything special at the time. I thought of it as just doing the job properly. Looking back, it was my first real lesson in a habit that's followed me since: something is either correct or it isn't, and it's your job to find out which before someone else does.

Working with corporate and high-value clients added a different layer — the cost of a mistake wasn't just financial, it was trust. Once someone's confidence in the process is shaken, it takes a long time to rebuild, longer than fixing whatever actually went wrong. That's stayed with me more than any specific procedure did.

I've since moved into QA and I'm working toward software engineering, alongside staying connected to accounting and finance — the field where I actually started. I don't think of it as having left banking behind so much as carrying it forward into whatever I do next. Five years there set the bar for what "done properly" means, long before I had a name for it.

I didn't come into any of this empty-handed. Amana is where I first learned what it costs to get something wrong.
